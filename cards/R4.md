# R4 — Eq (10), (12), (13): the DPP index, the virtual queue and the constrained policy

**Status: NOT FORMALIZED.**  There is no `TSDPP/R4.lean`, and no declaration in the artifact
corresponds to this card.

This card exists **because the card does not exist**.  Every other R-number has a file here; R4 is
the one node of the DAG that was named in `DESIGN.md` §3 and then never built.  Recording that
omission in the place a reader would look for the card is the honest disposition — an absent node
that leaves no trace reads as an oversight, whereas the same node with a card saying "deliberately
out of scope, here is what it would cost" is a scoped boundary.

## What the paper has here

| Eq | content |
|---|---|
| (10) | the DPP index of the belief state |
| (12) | the virtual queue `Z(t) = max{0, Z(t−1) + Σ_i π_i(t−1)c_i − B}` |
| (13) | the constrained policy `π*(t) = argmin_π V·Σ_i γ_i Ỹ_i(t) + Z(t)(Σ_i π_i(t)c_i − B)` |

Together with Eq (5)–(7) (the optimisation problem P1) and Eq (11) (the *observed* AoI `Ỹ`), these
are the four objects that turn "a policy" into "**the** paper's policy".  Eq (12) is what enforces
the budget `Σ_t Σ_i π_i(t)c_i ≤ B` in expectation, and Eq (13) is where the Lagrange multiplier
`Z` meets the index.

## Why it is not formalized

The artifact formalizes the paper's **analysis chain**, not its **problem statement**:

* the regret decomposition (Eq (27)–(35)) — `regret_decomp`, `penalty_diff`, `bestArm_congr`;
* the concentration events (Eq (38)–(40)) — `regret_le`, `eq39_collapse`, `tail_sum_le`;
* the resulting rate (Eq (19)) — `theorem2_concrete`, `theorem2_rate`.

That chain begins at *"let `π` be a policy whose per-round regret admits (27)"*.  The DPP index and
the virtual queue are upstream of that hypothesis, and closing them is a separate development: it
requires formalizing P1 as a constrained optimisation over policies, proving the DPP/carathéodory
index is optimal, and proving the virtual-queue policy is feasible for the budget constraint.

## What *is* formalized nearby, and how it differs

`TSDPP.bestArm` / `bestArm_spec` / `bestArm_congr` implement the **unconstrained** argmin of
Definition 4 — the belief-DPP policy with no Lagrange term.  That is Eq (13) **with `Z ≡ 0`**.  The
artifact's regret bound is therefore correctly read as a bound for the unconstrained policy, or
(equivalently) for any policy that supplies the decomposition as a hypothesis.

## Consequence for the fidelity claim

This is the largest single limitation recorded in `FIDELITY.md` (§1.0.1, and the verdict row in
§5), and it is a *scope* limitation rather than a defect in what was built.  The claim the artifact
is entitled to is stated there:

> *If* a policy admits the regret decomposition and the concentration events the paper derives for
> its policy, *then* its regret obeys the paper's rate.

It is **not** entitled to claim the paper's constrained policy attains that rate.  No declaration in
the artifact asserts otherwise: `Theorem2` carries `BR` and `J` as abstract reals satisfying
hypotheses, never as defined objects, so the un-formalized problem statement cannot leak into a
proved statement's meaning.
