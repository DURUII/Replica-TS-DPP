## Paper statement (Eq (31), p. 9, verbatim)

> **Lemma 5 (Concentration Inequality [45]).** If `X` has a beta distribution `Beta(α, β)` and `Y`
> is an independent copy of `X`, then, for all `0 ≤ r ≤ 1`, we have
> `Pr(|X − Y| ≥ r E(X)) ≤ 2e^{−αr²/8}`.

### The `r` range: the paper-level inconsistency **does** bite — [CORRECTED 2026-09-17, trap P-64]

The paper states the lemma for `0 ≤ r ≤ 1` but **uses** it at `r^l_i = max{1, √(8 log T/α^l_i)} ≥ 1`
(Eq (33)) — the ranges meet only at `r = 1`.  An earlier version of this card called this "not a
defect to repair", on the argument

> for `r ≥ 1`, `{|X−Y| ≥ rμ} ⊆ {|X−Y| ≥ μ}` and `e^{−cα} ≤ e^{−cαr²}`, so **`r = 1` implies
> every `r ≥ 1`**.

**That argument is invalid, and the claim it supports is false.**  The two displayed inequalities
both point the *same* way — each side of `Pr(|X−Y| ≥ rμ) ≤ 2e^{−cαr²}` decreases as `r` grows — and
a bound whose two sides move together is transferred by *neither* endpoint.  Written out, the chain
offered was `Pr(|X−Y| ≥ rμ) ≤ Pr(|X−Y| ≥ μ) ≤ 2e^{−cα}`, which is a bound by the **larger** right-hand
side `2e^{−cα}`; it does not yield `≤ 2e^{−cαr²}`.  Nor does it go the other way: the `r = 1` case is
the *loosest* one (`2e^{−cα} ≥ 1` for every `α ≤ 8 log 2 = 5.545`, hence vacuous), while large `r`
carries the strictest right-hand side.

The `r ≥ 1` claim is not merely unproved, it is **false**.  Exact rational arithmetic (each row
reproduced independently at 40 dps):

| `(α, β, r)` | `Pr(\|X−Y\| ≥ rα/(α+β))` exact | vs `11/100` | vs `1/8` |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1, 100, 10) | 2.809651246898e−05 | holds | **fails 3.77×** |
| (2, 200, 10) | 8.606640056681e−09 | **fails 15.43×** | fails 311× |
| (5, 500, 10) | 3.658586854855e−19 | **fails 1.4e5×** | fails 2.6e8× |

So the formalization does **not** "prove the `r = 1` core and derive `r ≥ 1` from it" — there is no
`r = 1` core in it, and the derivation was impossible.  The paper's Lemma 5 as printed
(`0 ≤ r ≤ 1`, `1/8`) is **true** — a sweep of `α ∈ [0.5, 200]`, `ρ ∈ [0.1, 1000]`, `r ∈ [0.1, 1]`
gives `min c_eff = 0.242848`, clearing `1/8` by +0.118 — but that range is **not where the paper uses
it**, and the artifact registered the use.

*Why this happens, and why it is not repairable here.*  The union-bound split `t₁ = μ(1+δ₁)`,
`t₂ = μδ` has gap `μ(1+δ₁−δ)`, so supplying the two *small* tails that a Chernoff bound needs
(`δ, δ₁ → 0`) forces `r ≥ 1` through the hypothesis `δ₁ ≤ r−1+δ`.  At the most favourable admissible
split for the witness `(2,200,10)` — `δ₁ = r−1 = 9`, `δ → 0` — the method gives
`A^α + B^α = 6.431e−7` against the required `2.789e−10`: a **2305× miss**.  For `r ≤ 1` the lower
tail must instead be paid at `δ ≈ 1−r/2`, where `B(ρ,δ)^α` is not exponentially small, and the bound
does not close at all.  **The method's usable range and the lemma's true range have disjoint
interiors**, so this card cannot be repaired by choosing a different constant, split, or ceiling.
See `../DESIGN.md` P-64 and `../FIDELITY.md` §3.4.

`../FIDELITY.md` and `../DESIGN.md` GAP-2b.

## Proof route

Work in the product measure `(betaMeasure α β).prod (betaMeasure α β)`. Write `μ := α/(α+β)`,
`ρ := β/α` (so `β = ρα`), `δ := ρs`.

1. **Symmetry + union bound.**  `{|X−Y| ≥ μ}` is the union of `{p.1 − p.2 ≥ μ}` and its mirror;
   the two are equimeasurable under `Measure.prod_swap` because both factors are the *same*
   measure, so it suffices to bound one and double.  Then
   `{p.1 − p.2 ≥ μ} ⊆ {p.1 ≥ μ(1+δ)} ∪ {p.2 ≤ μδ}`.
2. **The two one-sample tails**, each an exact Chernoff computation whose Γ-factors cancel.  The
   parameters are *forced* by the requirement that the elementary Gamma Laplace transform
   reproduce the Beta Chernoff exponent:
   `t₁ = μ(1+δ)`, `u = s(1+ρ)/((1+ρs)(1−s))`, giving `(1−u(1−t₁))^{−α}(1+ut₁)^{−β} = A(s)^α`
   with `A(s) = (1+ρs)(1−s)^ρ`; symmetrically `t₂ = μδ`,
   `v = (1−ρs)(1+ρ)/((1+ρ−ρs)ρs)`, giving `B(s)^α` with `B(s) = (ρs)(1/ρ+1−s)^ρ`.
3. **The witness** (`TSDPP/UnionBound.lean`, `s = min 1 (29/(50ρ))`) gives `A(s), B(s) ≤ e^{−c₀}`
   in both regimes; `s` must be non-existential to be evaluated.
4. **Two regimes, forced by `ρs < 1`.**  Regime 1 is `ρ > 29/50` **strictly**: `s = 29/(50ρ)`, so
   `ρs = 29/50 < 1` and `s < 1` — both `u` and `v` are finite and positive.  The endpoint
   `ρ = 29/50` belongs to regime 2, not here: there `s = 1` exactly, so `1 − s = 0` and `u` is
   **undefined**.  Getting the boundary on the wrong side is a genuine division by zero, not a
   stylistic choice.  Regime 2 is `ρ ≤ 29/50`: the witness is `s = 1`, where `v` is undefined, so
   that branch is **not** handled by the substitution at all — it uses `t₁ = μ(1+ρ) = 1`, whence
   `Pr(X ≥ 1) = 0` outright, and the lower tail reduces to the already-proved
   `B(1)^α = ρ^{(1−ρ)α}`.

   **Regime-1 side conditions have closed forms** (verified to `1e−49` over a 2000-point
   log-spaced grid on `ρ ∈ (29/50, 1000]`), so they are `norm_num`/`field_simp` rather than a
   real-arithmetic slog:

   | condition | closed form | discharged by |
   |---|---|---|
   | `u(1−t₁) < 1` | `u(1−t₁) = 29/79` **exactly, independent of `ρ`** | `norm_num` after the `have` |
   | `v·t₂ < 1` | `v·t₂ = (42/100)/(ρ + 42/100)` | `field_simp` + `linarith` from `0 < ρ` |
   | `v > 0` | `ρs = 29/50 < 1` | `norm_num` |
   | `u > 0`, `t₁ > 0`, `1 + u·t₁ > 0` | — | `positivity` |

   **Regime 2 is CLOSED, and the constant it uses is the "loose" one.**  Stated here because an
   earlier draft of this card pointed at the wrong one.

   *What the Lean actually proves.*  The `ρ ≤ 29/50` branch of `unionWitness`
   (`UnionBound.lean:112-131`) takes `s = 1`, reduces `ρ^(1−ρ)` via `mul_inv_rpow`, applies

### Why no `₁F₁` appears

`E[e^{λX}]` for `X ~ Beta(α,β)` **is** `₁F₁(α; α+β; λ)` — a confluent hypergeometric, absent from
Mathlib.  For the **union-bound route** this is the single reason the paper's `1/8` is lost: that
method must evaluate the two one-sample tails separately, and each evaluation needs `₁F₁`.  The
escape used here: the *product* of a Gamma Laplace transform at a positive argument and one at a
negative argument is elementary, and the Beta–Gamma ratio law (`TSDPP/BetaGamma.lean`) transports
the product measure to the Beta pair.  So the 2-D Chernoff that would need `₁F₁` is replaced by a
union bound over two 1-D Chernoffs that do not.

**That escape is what costs the constant — and a second route does not need `₁F₁` to exist at all.**
It is recorded in the next section.  Nothing there changes any declaration in this card: it is a
route under investigation, with one of its two halves proved.

### Route (D): compare the MGF coefficients *before* evaluating them — `(D+)` proved, `(D−)` open

Both halves are statements about `X ~ Beta(α,β)`, `c := α+β`.  Neither mentions `₁F₁` on the
*statement* side; the function appears only inside the proof, where it is a series.

> **(D+)**  For `α, β > 0` and `0 ≤ t < c`:  `E[e^{tX}] ≤ (1 − t/c)^{−α}`.

**Proof — elementary, term-by-term.**  With `(a)ₙ` the rising factorial,
`₁F₁(α;c;t) = Σₙ (α)ₙ/(c)ₙ · tⁿ/n!`, and `(1 − t/c)^{−α} = Σₙ (α)ₙ/cⁿ · tⁿ/n!` by the binomial
series.  Subtracting, the `n`-th term of the difference is `(α)ₙ tⁿ/n! · [1/(c)ₙ − 1/cⁿ]`, and
`(c)ₙ = c(c+1)⋯(c+n−1) ≥ cⁿ`, so **every term is ≤ 0** and the sum is ≤ 0.  ∎
The right-hand side is exactly the MGF of `Γ(α, rate c)` — the Gamma with the *same mean* as
`Beta(α,β)` — so `(D+)` reads: the Beta's MGF is dominated by that of the equal-mean Gamma.

> **(D−)**  For `α, β > 0` and `0 ≤ t < c`:  `E[e^{−tX}] ≤ (1 + t/c)^{−α}`.  — **OPEN, not proved.**

**Why the pair is exactly what is needed.**  Multiplying the two halves at `±t` gives
`(1 − t²/c²)^{−α}`, and `−log(1 − u²) ≤ 2u²` (valid for `u² ≤ 1/2`) turns that into `e^{2αt²/c²}`,
which is `BetaProdMGFBound` *verbatim*.  Markov at `t = r(α+β)/4` then gives the exponent exactly
`−αr²/8`, and the hypothesis `t ≤ c/4` is exactly `r ≤ 1` — the paper's own range.  So
`(D+) ∧ (D−)` yields the paper's Lemma 5 **at the paper's own constant `1/8`**, which the
union-bound route provably cannot reach.

**Numerics — evidence, not proof.**  `₁F₁(a;c;−t)` was evaluated as `e^{−t}·₁F₁(b;c;t)` (Kummer),
so that *both* series have only positive terms and no cancellation can occur; 300-digit `mpmath`.

| sweep | cells | `(D+)` worst log-ratio | `(D−)` worst log-ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a,b ∈ {0.01…1000}` × `t/c ∈ {0.05, 0.15, 0.24, 0.25}` | 400 | `−1.397278e−8` at `(0.01, 1000, 0.05)` | `−1.1441111e−8`, same cell |
| small `α`: `a ∈ {0.001…1}` × `b ∈ {0.001…10⁶}` | 224 | `−1.386425005e−12` at `(0.001, 10⁶, 0.05)` | `−1.134919639e−12`, same cell |

No violation in either.  A range probe at step `0.02` found `(D−)` still holding at **`u = 0.96`** —
the top of the scanned range, on all 8 sampled `(α,β)` pairs — so `t ≤ c/4` is **not** what `(D−)`
requires; it enters only from the paper's `r ≤ 1` tilt and from `−log(1 − u²) ≤ 2u²`.  The worst
cell for *both* halves is the **smallest `u`** sampled, where both reduce to `φ₂u²` with
`φ₂ = α(α+1)/(2(c+1)) ≥ 0` — consistent with a `(D−)` true for all `t < c`, not on a restricted
range.  A `(D−)` proof is being sought; the `α≈52`/`hnum` obstruction is *not* inherited by it.

*Two numerical traps met here.*  **(i)** `₁F₁(a;c;−t)` by direct series at `a ≫ b` cancels
catastrophically: at `(1000, 0.1, 0.25)` it returned `−8.4e−95` for a quantity that is `≈3.7e−109`,
and a first pass reported a `(D−)` **violation** of `7.82` in the log-ratio that does not exist.
The tell was a separate anomaly list — the sweep had been printing "negative-valued `₁F₁`" for 12
cells.  Kummer removes the cancellation entirely.  **(ii)** Kummer is `₁F₁(a;c;t) = eᵗ·₁F₁(c−a;c;−t)`:
it helps the *negative* argument and must not be applied blindly to the positive one, where the
direct series is already positive-term.

#### Independent check of the convex-order route's load-bearing lemma

A proof of `(D−)` has been proposed by way of **convex order** `X ≤_cx Y`, `X ~ Beta(a,b)`,
`Y ~ Γ(a, rate a+b)`.  Its load-bearing step is a statement about the *survival* gap
```
h(x) := P(X > x) − P(Y > x)   has exactly one sign change on (0,∞), and it is + → −.
```
Verified numerically, independently, for `α` a positive **integer** and `β` any positive real.
The verification order is **anchors → grid → magnitude filter → verdict**, and it matters: four
successive runs gave four mutually contradictory answers, and **three of the four disagreements
were faults in the checker, not in the claim** (wrong tail written — `₁F₁(1;2;w) = (e^w−1)/w`
collapses the intended upper tail to `1 − e^{−cs}`; an unsorted grid whose `0.1 → 0.002` back-jump
manufactured a change at every crossing; a grid hole on `(0.1, 1.0)` that postponed crossing
*locations*; and a self-check "expected value" that was itself wrong).  Each is recorded as
`P-19`…`P-22` in `TSDPP/Lemma5PaperRange.TRAPS.md`.

Acceptance criteria actually used: `SX − SY` must equal the elementary closed form **bit-for-bit**
at working precision (not "to within") — the three anchors `(1,1)`, `(1,100)`, `(1,1000)` all have
one; the grid must be strictly increasing and gap-free (1461 points, `1e-6 → 10`); samples with
`|h| < 1e-45` must not be scored.  Under those, **20 of 20 cases** show exactly one `+ → −`
crossing, and the crossing position matches the closed form — for `(a,b) = (1,1)` the two roots
agree to all 25 digits, `0.7968121300200200461615209`.

What this does **and does not** establish.  It establishes that the lemma is not a numerical
artifact, and that the counterexamples the earlier faulty checkers produced do not exist.  It does
**not** prove the lemma: the closed-form evaluators need integer `α`, so real `α < 1` — precisely
the regime `(D−)` is needed in — is outside the anchorable range, and no numerical agreement is a
proof.  The proof itself remains to be written, and with it the passage from the sign change to
`E[e^{−tX}] ≤ E[e^{−tY}]` via the stop-loss representation.

#### (D+) and (D−) are one inequality, and one sufficient condition covers both

Write `m(t) := (log M)'(t) = E_t[X]`, the mean of `X` under the exponential tilt with parameter `t`
— no hypothesis needed, this is the definition of the tilt. Define `ψ(t) := (c − t)·m(t)`. Two
identities are definitional:

```
   (log R)'(t) = (ψ(t) − a)/(c − t),        ψ(0) = a,        ψ' = −E_t[X] + (c − t)·Var_t(X),
```

the second because `m' = (log M)'' = Var_t(X)`. Since `c − t > 0` on `t < c`, the sign of `(log R)'`
is the sign of `ψ − a`, and `ψ(0) = a`. Hence

> **`(C')` `ψ` nonincreasing on `(−c,c)`** ⟺ **`(c − t)·Var_t(X) ≤ E_t(X)`**
> ⟹ `R` decreases on `(0,c)` and increases on `(−c,0)` ⟹ `R(t) ≤ R(0) = 1` on `(−c,c)`.

**The equality case is identified, and it is the Gamma.** For `W ~ Γ(a, rate c)` — whose MGF is
exactly `(1 − t/c)^{−a}`, i.e. the right-hand side of both `(D+)` and `(D−)` — one has
`E_t[W] = a/(c−t)` and `Var_t(W) = a/(c−t)²`, so `(c−t)·Var_t(W) = E_t(W)` **with equality**, and
`ψ_Γ ≡ a` is constant. `(C')` therefore reads: *the Beta's tilt-mean decays at least as fast as the
equal-mean Gamma's*, and the Gamma sits exactly on the boundary.

**Numerics, and the reading of them.** 1120 cells (`a ∈ {0.001…100}`, `b ∈ {0.001…10⁵}`,
`u = t/c ∈ ±{0.01…0.99}`), `ψ` by central differences of a cancellation-free `log M` at 100 digits,
step `10⁻⁸`: **0 violations**, worst `ψ' = −1.270852592e−16` at `(0.001, 10⁵, −0.99)`; worst
`log R = −4.906333904e−13` on the same grid. The worst cell sits **on the finite-difference noise
floor** — `ψ'` there is pressed against `0`, not comfortably below it — and it is the degenerate
corner `a → 0, b → ∞` that trap P-18 already identified for `(C)` itself. So `(C')` is *not* a
slack-carrying sufficient condition: it is as tight as `(D±)` in that corner, and a proof must be
exact there, not "a coarse inequality plus margin" (the P-16 warning, now applying to `(C')` too).

#### A second route that also collapses — convex order, and the bracket it dies on

Since `e^{tx}` is convex for **every** real `t`, the convex order `Beta(a,b) ≤_cx Γ(a, rate c)`
(a stop-loss order, with the means already equal at `a/c`) would give `(D±)` in one line. It is
strictly stronger than `(D±)`, and it reduces to:

> `EL(k) := ∫₀^k (F_X − F_W)(s) ds ≤ 0` for all `k ∈ (0,1)`.

Expanding both CDFs in powers of `s` (binomial series for `(1−st)^{b−1}`; `γ(a,x)` series for the
Gamma) gives, with `c = a+b`,

> `F_X(s) − F_W(s) = (s^a/Γ(a+1))·Σ_{n=0}^{b−1} (−1)ⁿ wₙ sⁿ`,   `wₙ = (a)ₙ|βₙ| / ((a+1)ₙ n!) > 0`,
> `βₙ := Γ(a+b)/Γ(b−n) − c^{a+n}`.

**Every `βₙ < 0`**, and the proof is one line of convexity of `log Γ`:

> `ψ(x) < log x` for `x > 0` (convexity of `log Γ`, plus the difference quotient
> `log Γ(x+1) − log Γ(x) = log x`), hence
> `log[Γ(t+θ)/Γ(t)] = ∫_t^{t+θ} ψ < ∫_t^{t+θ} log s ds < θ·log(t+θ)`, i.e.
> **`Γ(t+θ)/Γ(t) < (t+θ)^θ`** for all `t, θ > 0`; take `t = b−n`, `θ = a+n`.

Equivalently by AM–GM: `Γ(a+b)/Γ(b−n)` is the product of the `a+n` consecutive integers
`(b−n)(b−n+1)···(b+a−1)`, whose arithmetic mean is
`b + [a(a−1)/2 − n(n+1)/2]/(a+n) < a + b`, so the product is below `(a+b)^{a+n}`.

**This series route does not close convex order**, and the reason is the point: `F_X − F_W` is again a
**positive-weight alternating series** — the `n = 0` term dominates at small `s` (giving the right
sign), but near `k → 1` the sign of the alternating sum is not decidable by any elementary
criterion, because `wₙ` is *not* monotone (`w_{n+1}/wₙ ≈ (a+b)/(n+1)`: first increasing, then
decreasing, so Leibniz does not apply).

**But convex order is nevertheless TRUE and proved — by a route that never forms that series.**
The density ratio `f_X/f_Y = K·e^{cx}(1−x)^{b−1}` is elementary (`K < 1` by `Γ(t+θ)/Γ(t) < (t+θ)^θ`,
which this section already proves), and `h' = f_Y(1−r)` turns the sign question into "where does `r`
cross `1`". The step that looked like it needed the hard estimate — `h(x₂) < 0` — is pure
monotonicity plus `h(1) = −S_Y(1) < 0`. See **`L5-CONVEX.md`** for the theorem, the full DAG and the
corollaries: `(D+)`, `(D−)` on their full ranges, `BetaProdMGFBound`, and the paper's own Lemma 5 at
its own `1/8` on `0 ≤ r ≤ 1`.

#### Three routes, one obstruction — and one route that does not pass through it

`(D−)`, `(C')` and convex order all reduce to a *sign question for a positive-weight alternating
series*, **if one insists on evaluating the objects involved**:

| route | the series whose sign is needed |
|---|---|
| `(D−)` | `Σ_{n≥2} φₙ(−u)ⁿ ≥ 0`, `φₙ = (a)ₙ(1 − cⁿ/(c)ₙ)/n! > 0` |
| `(C')` | `ψ' = −E_t[X] + (c−t)Var_t(X) ≤ 0` (a two-term difference, degenerate-corner-tight) |
| convex order, via the CDF series | `Σ_{n≥0}(−1)ⁿ wₙ kⁿ ≤ 0`, `wₙ > 0` |

In all three the leading term has the right sign and the difficulty is entirely in the tail. Whoever
supplies a usable sufficient condition for "positive-weight alternating series has this sign" and
verifies the weights satisfy it closes **all three at once**.

That is still true, and it is now moot. The way through is to **not evaluate** — to compare the
*densities* instead of the CDFs or the MGFs, where the shared `x^{a−1}` factor cancels and the
remaining ratio is a one-line elementary function with a single critical point. The obstruction was a
property of the chosen expansion, not of the problem:

* convex order: **closed** by the density-ratio route (`L5-CONVEX.md`);
* `(C')`: now a **corollary** of convex order via `ψ' = −E_t[X] + (c−t)Var_t(X)`, not an independent
  conjecture — its zero numerical slack in the degenerate corner is a property of a proved statement;
* `(D+)` and `(D−)`: **corollaries** of convex order on their full ranges, `(D−)` for all `t ≥ 0`.

### Independent falsification of the *final statement* (not the proof route)

The gates above check proofs, not truths, so the statement was falsified directly — `Pr(|X−Y| ≥ t)`
computed by the exact 1-D reduction `∫ f(y)·[1 − F(y+t) + F(y−t)] dy` (validated: `Beta(1,1)` at
`t = 1/2` gives `0.250000000000` and at `t = 0.2` gives `0.64`, both exact):

* **50 grid points** over 10 `(α,β)` pairs × 5 values of `r ≥ 1`: **zero violations**, max
  `LHS/RHS = 0.1997`.  (Includes the asymmetric and small-shape cases `Beta(1,4)`, `Beta(0.5,0.5)`,
  `Beta(3,7)`, and the concentrated `Beta(50,50)`.)
* **Tight-corner scan** over `13 × 8` `(α,β)` pairs at `r = 1`: sup ratio `0.2502` at
  `(α,β) = (0.01, 0.01)`.
* **Why the supremum is `1/4`, in closed form.** As `α,β → 0` jointly at fixed `μ = α/(α+β)`,
  `Beta(α,β)` degenerates to `Bernoulli(μ)`, so `|X−Y| ∈ {0,1}` and
  `Pr(|X−Y| ≥ μ) → 2μ(1−μ) ≤ 1/2`, attained at `μ = 1/2`; the right-hand side is `2e^{−c₀α} → 2`.
  Ratio `→ 1/4`.  In the opposite regime (`α,β` large, Gaussian tail) the ratio is
  `≈ exp(−r²α[(1+ρ)/(4ρ) − c₀])` with `ρ = β/α`, and `(1+ρ)/(4ρ) → 1/4 > c₀ = 0.11` as `ρ → ∞`,
  so the exponent stays negative.  **The two regimes meet at the same `1/4` bound.**  Since `r ≥ 1`
  forces `r²α ≥ α`, the small-`α` corner is the binding one.

So the formalized statement carries a **factor-4 slack** over the truth at its worst point — it is
not tight, and the `c₀ = 11/100` substitution is safe at the *statement* level, independently of
whether the union-bound route is the best available proof.

*Caveat, stated rather than hidden:* the `α < 0.01` corner produces scipy `IntegrationWarning`s
(roundoff in the extrapolation table), so those individual values are noisy.  The `1/4` claim does
not rest on them — it is the closed-form degeneration argument above, which the numerics confirm.
